<li>DeepSeek&#8217;s powerful, cheap AI models have taken the tech world by storm.</li>
<li>Altman said OpenAI will embrace one of DeepSeek&#8217;s popular approaches.</li>
<li>The CEO said OpenAI has &#8220;been on the wrong side of history&#8221; when it comes to model weights.</li>
</ul>
<p>When rivals take a different approach and succeed, it sometimes pays to change course. </p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>This is what <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-deepseek-ai-model-distillation-training-data-copyright-karma-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">Sam Altman</a> said OpenAI will do, according to a Reddit AMA session on Friday. </p>
<p>The discussion touched on several AI topics, but in particular Altman was asked about DeepSeek, which has taken the tech world by storm after rolling out top-performing AI models that are relatively <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/chinese-ai-lab-deepseek-massively-undercuts-openai-on-pricing-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">cheap to use</a>. </p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>One Reddit user asked if <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-ai-adjusted-earnings-groupon-wework-accounting-2024-10" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">OpenAI</a> could show &#8220;all of the thinking tokens.&#8221; This refers to the chain of thought that new &#8220;reasoning&#8221; <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-peak-data-google-deepmind-researchers-solution-test-time-compute-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI</a> models use to break tasks into smaller steps — similar to how humans think through complex challenges. </p>
<p>OpenAI&#8217;s o1 and o3 models use this reasoning approach, however they don&#8217;t show any of the intermediate thinking steps to users, and instead just show the final answer. </p>
<p>DeepSeek&#8217;s reasoning models, such as its R1 offering, show every step to users. When Business Insider <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/deepseek-demo-chinese-ai-model-math-reasoning-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">demoed DeepSeek</a> with the Chinese lab&#8217;s DeepThink setting, it shared about 16 pages of mathematical steps before providing the correct answer to a tough question. </p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>On Friday, Altman said OpenAI would follow DeepSeek&#8217;s approach. &#8220;Yeah we are gonna show a much more helpful and detailed version of this, soon. Credit to R1 for updating us,&#8221; he wrote. </p>
<h2 id="14e509e6-72ec-4193-a123-3c482cb4de9d" data-toc-id="14e509e6-72ec-4193-a123-3c482cb4de9d">Open-source and open weights</h2>
<p>Meta chief AI scientist Yann LeCun has said the biggest takeaway from DeepSeek&#8217;s success is the value of <a target="_blank" rel="" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/mark-zuckerberg-open-source-ai-platforms-future-competition-apple-llama-2024-9" data-analytics-product-module="body_link"><u>open-source</u></a> AI models versus proprietary ones. </p>
<p>Meta&#8217;s Llama models are mostly open-source, letting anyone access important details such as weights and parameters for free. Sharing the inner-workings of models like this allows other developers and many companies to customize these models for their own use. </p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Despite its name, OpenAI has taken a more closed approach to AI development so far. Most of its models are proprietary and the startup charges for access. </p>
<p>During the Reddit AMA on Friday, <span>Altman was asked if OpenAI would consider releasing some of its model weights, and publishing some research.</span></p>
<p><span>&#8220;Yes, we are discussing. I personally think we have been on the wrong side of history here and need to figure out a different open source strategy; not everyone at OpenAI shares this view, and it&#8217;s also not our current highest priority,&#8221; Altman replied. </span></p>
