<p>Strict AI regulations to tackle deepfakes and ensure transparency.</p>
<p></em></p></div>
<div>
<p id="Spainsgovernment">Spain’s government has approved a <a href="https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/spain-impose-massive-fines-not-labelling-ai-generated-content-2025-03-11/" data-type="link" data-id="https://www.reuters.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/spain-impose-massive-fines-not-labelling-ai-generated-content-2025-03-11/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow">bill imposing heavy fines</a> on companies that fail to label AI-generated content, aiming to combat the spread of deepfakes. </p>
<p id="Thelegislation">The legislation, which aligns with the European Union’s AI Act, classifies non-compliance as a serious offence, with penalties reaching up to €35 million or 7% of a company’s global revenue.</p>
<p id="DigitalTransformation">Digital Transformation Minister Oscar Lopez stressed that AI can be a force for good but also a tool for misinformation and threats to democracy. </p>
<p id="Thebill">The bill also bans manipulative AI techniques, such as subliminal messaging targeting vulnerable groups, and restricts the use of AI-driven biometric profiling, except in cases of national security.</p>
<p id="Spainis">Spain is one of the first EU nations to implement these strict AI regulations, going beyond the looser <a href="https://dig.watch/countries/united-states-of-america" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener">US</a> approach, which relies on voluntary compliance. </p>
<p id="Anewly">A newly established AI supervisory agency, AESIA, will oversee enforcement, alongside sector-specific regulators handling privacy, financial markets, and law enforcement concerns.</p>
<p id="Formore"><em>For more information on these topics, visit <a href="https://www.diplomacy.edu/" target="_blank" rel="noreferrer noopener nofollow">diplomacy.edu</a>.</em></p>
