<div>
<p>As the 2025 AI Action Summit kicks off in Paris, global leaders, industry experts, and academics are converging to address the challenges and opportunities presented by AI.</p>
<p>Against the backdrop of rapid technological advancements and growing societal concerns, the summit aims to build on the progress made since the 2024 <a href="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/uk-and-south-korea-cohost-ai-seoul-summit/">Seoul Safety Summit</a> and establish a cohesive global framework for AI governance.  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">AI Action Summit is ‘a wake-up call’</h3>
<p>French President Emmanuel Macron has described the summit as “a wake-up call for Europe,” emphasising the need for collective action in the face of AI’s transformative potential. This comes as the US has committed $500 billion to AI infrastructure.</p>
<p>The UK, meanwhile, has unveiled its <a href="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/uk-government-signs-off-sweeping-ai-action-plan/">Opportunities Action Plan</a> ahead of the full implementation of the UK AI Act. Ahead of the AI Summit, UK tech minister Peter Kyle told <a href="https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/feb/10/artifical-intelligence-peter-kyle-ai-summit-paris">The Guardian</a> the AI race must be led by “western, liberal, democratic” countries.</p>
<p>These developments signal a renewed global dedication to harnessing AI’s capabilities while addressing its risks.  </p>
<p>Matt Cloke, CTO at <a href="https://www.endava.com/">Endava</a>, highlighted the importance of bridging the gap between AI’s potential and its practical implementation.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignright size-full is-resized"><img fetchpriority="high" fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" width="727" height="727" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit.jpg" alt="Headshot of Matt Cloke." class="wp-image-104265" style="width:176px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit.jpg 727w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpg 400w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/matt-cloke-ai-action-summit-600x600.jpg 600w" sizes="(max-width: 727px) 100vw, 727px"/></figure>
<p>“Much of the conversation is set to focus on understanding the risks involved with using AI while helping to guide decision-making in an ever-evolving landscape,” he said.  </p>
<p>Cloke also stressed the role of organisations in ensuring AI adoption goes beyond regulatory frameworks.</p>
<p>“Modernising core systems enables organisations to better harness AI while ensuring regulatory compliance,” he explained.</p>
<p>“With improved data management, automation, and integration capabilities, these systems make it easier for organisations to stay agile and quickly adapt to impending regulatory changes.”  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Governance and workforce among critical AI Action Summit topics</h3>
<p>Kit Cox, CTO and Founder of <a href="https://www.enate.io/">Enate</a>, outlined three critical areas for the summit’s agenda.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignleft size-full is-resized"><img decoding="async" width="713" height="713" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit.jpeg" alt="Headshot of Kit Cox ahead of the 2025 AI Action Summit in Paris." class="wp-image-104264" style="width:176px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit.jpeg 713w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpeg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpeg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpeg 400w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/kit-cox-ai-action-summit-600x600.jpeg 600w" sizes="(max-width: 713px) 100vw, 713px"/></figure>
<p>“First, AI governance needs urgent clarity,” he said. “We must establish global guidelines to ensure AI is safe, ethical, and aligned across nations. A disconnected approach won’t work; we need unity to build trust and drive long-term progress.”</p>
<p>Cox also emphasised the need for a future-ready workforce.</p>
<p>“Employers and governments must invest in upskilling the workforce for an AI-driven world,” he said. “This isn’t just about automation replacing jobs; it’s about creating opportunities through education and training that genuinely prepare people for the future of work.”  </p>
<p>Finally, Cox called for democratising AI’s benefits.</p>
<p>“AI must be fair and democratic both now and in the future,” he said. “The benefits can’t be limited to a select few. We must ensure that AI’s power reaches beyond Silicon Valley to all corners of the globe, creating opportunities for everyone to thrive.”  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Developing AI in the public interest</h3>
<p>Professor Gina Neff, Professor of Responsible AI at <a href="https://www.qmul.ac.uk/">Queen Mary University of London</a> and Executive Director at Cambridge University’s <a href="https://www.mctd.ac.uk/">Minderoo Centre for Technology &amp; Democracy</a>, stressed the importance of making AI relatable to everyday life.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignright size-full is-resized"><img decoding="async" width="740" height="740" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit.jpg" alt="Headshot of Professor Gina Neff." class="wp-image-104263" style="width:175px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit.jpg 740w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpg 400w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gina-neff-ai-action-summit-600x600.jpg 600w" sizes="(max-width: 740px) 100vw, 740px"/></figure>
<p>“For us in civil society, it’s essential that we bring imaginaries about AI into the everyday,” she said. “From the barista who makes your morning latte to the mechanic fixing your car, they all have to understand how AI impacts them and, crucially, why AI is a human issue.”  </p>
<p>Neff also pushed back against big tech’s dominance in AI development.</p>
<p>“I’ll be taking this spirit of public interest into the Summit and pushing back against big tech’s push for hyperscaling. Thinking about AI as something we’re building together – like we do our cities and local communities – puts us all in a better place.”</p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Addressing bias and building equitable AI</h3>
<p>Professor David Leslie, Professor of Ethics, Technology, and Society at Queen Mary University of London, highlighted the unresolved challenges of bias and diversity in AI systems.</p>
<p>“Over a year after the first <a href="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/uk-reveals-ai-safety-summit-opening-day-agenda/">AI Safety Summit</a> at Bletchley Park, only incremental progress has been made to address the many problems of cultural bias and toxic and imbalanced training data that have characterised the development and use of Silicon Valley-led frontier AI systems,” he said.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignleft size-full is-resized"><img loading="lazy" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="500" height="500" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/David-Leslie-ai-action-summit.jpg" alt="Headshot of Professor David Leslie ahead of the 2025 AI Action Summit in Paris." class="wp-image-104262" style="width:179px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/David-Leslie-ai-action-summit.jpg 500w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/David-Leslie-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/David-Leslie-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/David-Leslie-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 500px) 100vw, 500px"/></figure>
<p>Leslie called for a renewed focus on public interest AI.</p>
<p>“The French AI Action Summit promises to refocus the conversation on AI governance to tackle these and other areas of immediate risk and harm,” he explained. “A main focus will be to think about how to advance public interest AI for all through mission-driven and society-led funding.”  </p>
<p>He proposed the creation of a public interest AI foundation, supported by governments, companies, and philanthropic organisations.</p>
<p>“This type of initiative will have to address issues of algorithmic and data biases head on, at concrete and practice-based levels,” he said. “Only then can it stay true to the goal of making AI technologies – and the infrastructures upon which they depend – accessible global public goods.”  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Systematic evaluation  </h3>
<p>Professor Maria Liakata, Professor of Natural Language Processing at Queen Mary University of London, emphasised the need for rigorous evaluation of AI systems.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignright size-full is-resized"><img loading="lazy" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="450" height="450" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/maria-liakata-ai-action-summit.jpeg" alt="Headshot of Professor Maria Liakata ahead of the 2025 AI Action Summit in Paris." class="wp-image-104261" style="width:172px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/maria-liakata-ai-action-summit.jpeg 450w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/maria-liakata-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpeg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/maria-liakata-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpeg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/maria-liakata-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 450px) 100vw, 450px"/></figure>
<p>“AI has the potential to make public service more efficient and accessible,” she said. “But at the moment, we are not evaluating AI systems properly. Regulators are currently on the back foot with evaluation, and developers have no systematic way of offering the evidence regulators need.”  </p>
<p>Liakata called for a flexible and systematic approach to AI evaluation.</p>
<p>“We must remain agile and listen to the voices of all stakeholders,” she said. “This would give us the evidence we need to develop AI regulation and help us get there faster. It would also help us get better at anticipating the risks posed by AI.”  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">AI in healthcare: Balancing innovation and ethics</h3>
<p>Dr Vivek Singh, Lecturer in Digital Pathology at <a href="https://www.bci.qmul.ac.uk/">Barts Cancer Institute</a>, Queen Mary University of London, highlighted the ethical implications of AI in healthcare.</p>
<figure class="wp-block-image alignleft size-full is-resized"><img loading="lazy" loading="lazy" decoding="async" width="600" height="600" src="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/vivek-singh-ai-action-summit.jpg" alt="Headshot of Dr Vivek Singh ahead of the 2025 AI Action Summit in Paris." class="wp-image-104260" style="width:179px;height:auto" srcset="https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/vivek-singh-ai-action-summit.jpg 600w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/vivek-singh-ai-action-summit-300x300.jpg 300w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/vivek-singh-ai-action-summit-150x150.jpg 150w, https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/vivek-singh-ai-action-summit-400x400.jpg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 600px) 100vw, 600px"/></figure>
<p>“The Paris AI Action Summit represents a critical opportunity for global collaboration on AI governance and innovation,” he said. “I hope to see actionable commitments that balance ethical considerations with the rapid advancement of AI technologies, ensuring they benefit society as a whole.”  </p>
<p>Singh called for clear frameworks for international cooperation.</p>
<p>“A key outcome would be the establishment of clear frameworks for international cooperation, fostering trust and accountability in AI development and deployment,” he said.  </p>
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">AI Action Summit: A pivotal moment</h3>
<p>The 2025 AI Action Summit in Paris represents a pivotal moment for global AI governance. With calls for unity, equity, and public interest at the forefront, the summit aims to address the challenges of bias, regulation, and workforce readiness while ensuring AI’s benefits are shared equitably.</p>
<p>As world leaders and industry experts converge, the hope is that actionable commitments will pave the way for a more inclusive and ethical AI future.</p>
