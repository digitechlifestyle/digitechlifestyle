<div class="group">
<p>LONDON — Synthesia, a video platform that uses artificial intelligence to generate clips featuring multilingual human avatars, has raised $180 million in an investment round valuing the startup at $2.1 billion.</p>
<p>That&#8217;s more than than double the $1 billion Synthesia was worth in its last financing in 2023.</p>
<p>The London-based startup said Wednesday that the funding round was led by venture firm NEA with participation from Atlassian Ventures, World Innovation Lab and PSP Growth.</p>
<p>NEA counts <span class="QuoteInBody-quoteNameContainer" data-test="QuoteInBody" id="RegularArticle-QuoteInBody-1"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/UBER/">Uber</a><span class="QuoteInBody-inlineButton"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistContainer" id="-WatchlistDropdown" data-analytics-id="-WatchlistDropdown"><button class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistButton" aria-label="Add To Watchlist" data-testid="dropdown-btn"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-addWatchListFromTag"/></button></span></span></span> and TikTok parent company ByteDance among its portfolio companies. Synthesia is also backed by chip giant <span class="QuoteInBody-quoteNameContainer" data-test="QuoteInBody" id="RegularArticle-QuoteInBody-2"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/NVDA/">Nvidia</a><span class="QuoteInBody-inlineButton"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistContainer" id="-WatchlistDropdown" data-analytics-id="-WatchlistDropdown"><button class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistButton" aria-label="Add To Watchlist" data-testid="dropdown-btn"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-addWatchListFromTag"/></button></span></span></span>.</p>
<p>Victor Riparbelli, CEO of Synthesia, told CNBC that investors appraised the businesses differently from other companies in the space due to its focus on &#8220;utility.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;Of course, the hype cycle is beneficial to us,&#8221; Riparbelli said in an interview. &#8220;For us, what&#8217;s important is building an actually good business.&#8221;</p>
<p>Synthesia isn&#8217;t &#8220;dependent&#8221; on venture capital — as opposed to companies like OpenAI, Anthropic and Mistral, Riparbelli added.</p>
<p>These startups have raised billions of dollars at eye-watering valuations while burning through sizable amounts of money to train and develop their foundational AI models.</p>
</div>

<div class="group">
<p>Synthesia&#8217;s not the only startup shaking up the world of video production with AI. Other startups offer solutions for producing and editing video content with AI, like <a href="http://veed.io/" target="_blank">Veed.io</a> and Runway.</p>
<p>Meanwhile, the likes of OpenAI and <span class="QuoteInBody-quoteNameContainer" data-test="QuoteInBody" id="RegularArticle-QuoteInBody-4"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/ADBE/">Adobe</a><span class="QuoteInBody-inlineButton"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistContainer" id="-WatchlistDropdown" data-analytics-id="-WatchlistDropdown"><button class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistButton" aria-label="Add To Watchlist" data-testid="dropdown-btn"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-addWatchListFromTag"/></button></span></span></span> have also developed generative AI tools for video creation.</p>
<p>Eric Liaw, a London-based partner at VC firm IVP, told CNBC that companies at the application layer of AI haven&#8217;t garnered as much investor hype as firms in the infrastructure layer.</p>
<p>&#8220;The amount of money that the application layer companies need to raise isn&#8217;t as large — and therefore the valuations aren&#8217;t necessarily as eye popping&#8221; as companies like Nvidia,&#8221; Liaw told CNBC last month.</p>
<p>Riparbelli said that money raised from the latest financing round would be used to invest in &#8220;more of the same,&#8221; furthering product development and investing more into security and compliance.</p>
<p>Last year, Synthesia made a series of updates to its platform, including the ability to produce AI avatars using a laptop webcam or phone, full-body avatars with arms and hands and a screen recording tool that has an AI avatar guide users through what they&#8217;re viewing.</p>
<p>On the AI safety front, in October Synthesia conducted a <a href="https://www.synthesia.io/post/nist-red-team-test-content-deepfakes" target="_blank">public red team test</a> for risks around online harms, which demonstrated how the firm&#8217;s compliance controls counter attempts to create non-consensual deepfakes of people or use its avatars to encourage suicide, adult content or gambling.</p>
<p>The National Institute of Standards and Technology test was led by Rumman Chowdhury, a renowned data scientist who was formerly head of AI ethics at Twitter — before it became known as X under <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/elon-musk/">Elon Musk</a>.</p>
<p>Riparbelli said that Synthesia is seeing increased interest from large enterprise customers, particularly in the U.S., thanks to its focus on security and compliance.</p>
<p>More than half of Synthesia&#8217;s annual revenue now comes from customers in the U.S., while Europe accounts for almost half.</p>
<p>Synthesia has also been ramping up hiring. The company recently tapped former Amazon executive Peter Hill as its chief technology officer. The company now employs over 400 people globally.</p>
<p>Synthesia&#8217;s announcement follows the unveiling of Prime Minister Keir Starmer&#8217;s <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/12/britain-wants-to-become-global-ai-leader-build-domestic-openai-rival.html">50-point plan to make the U.K. a global leader in AI</a>.</p>
<p>U.K. Technology Minister Peter Kyle said the investment &#8220;showcases the confidence investors have in British tech&#8221; and &#8220;highlights the global leadership of U.K.-based companies in pioneering generative AI innovations.&#8221;</p>
