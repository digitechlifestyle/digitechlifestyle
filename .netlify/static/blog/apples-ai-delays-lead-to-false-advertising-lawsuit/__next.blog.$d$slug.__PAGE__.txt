<p><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.apple.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">Apple</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true"> has been sued for false advertising due to its delayed artificial intelligence (AI) rollout.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">As Axios </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.axios.com/2025/03/20/apple-suit-false-advertising-ai-intelligence?utm_source=flipboard&amp;utm_content=other" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">reported</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">, the proposed class action suit, filed last week in federal court, seeks unspecified damages for customers who purchased Apple products outfitted with the company’s AI capabilities, dubbed “Apple Intelligence.”</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">“Apple’s advertisements saturated the internet, television, and other airwaves to cultivate a clear and reasonable consumer expectation that these transformative features would be available upon the iPhone’s release,” the suit says.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">“This drove unprecedented excitement in the market, even for Apple, as the company knew it would, and as part of Apple’s ongoing effort to convince consumers to upgrade at a premium price and to distinguish itself from competitors deemed to be winning the AI-arms race.”</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">However, the suit adds, Apple’s products featured “a significantly limited or entirely absent version of Apple Intelligence,” thus misleading consumers. The suit also says Apple advertised its products based on “overstated </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.pymnts.com/tag/apple-intelligence/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">AI capabilities</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">.”</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">The lawsuit also notes that Apple has pulled a recent ad campaign touting </span><span data-preserver-spaces="true">AI</span><span data-preserver-spaces="true"> capabilities of its Siri voice assistant from YouTube, but has otherwise “failed to retract all the similarly false representations in the market that began in the summer of 2024.”</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">PYMNTS has contacted Apple for comment but has not yet gotten a reply.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">The suit comes at a time when — as noted here last week — Apple is struggling to catch up to competitors such as Amazon and Google as those companies roll out more advanced AI features.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">The company has said that improvements to Siri — originally planned to debut this year — had been pushed back until 2026. Amazon, meanwhile, recently announced Alexa+, an updated version of its home voice assistant that </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.pymnts.com/amazon-alexa/2025/amazon-unveils-an-agentic-smarter-and-more-capable-alexa-plus/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">embeds generative and agentic AI</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">“It is a remarkable fall from grace for Siri that has culminated in a rare move by Apple: </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.pymnts.com/apple/2025/report-apple-makes-leadership-changes-to-speed-development-of-ai-powered-siri/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">demoting the executive</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true"> in charge,” PYMNTS wrote.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">The executive in question is </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.apple.com/leadership/john-giannandrea/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">John Giannandrea</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">, ex-head of search and AI at Google, who had been overseeing Siri’s upgrades before. That job now goes to Vision Pro creator </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/fortune-india_apple-shakes-up-ai-leadership-vision-pro-activity-7308754110931972096-A1k2" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">Mike Rockwell</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">Speaking with PYMNTS earlier this month, Siri co-designer Luc Julia said that an obsession with perfection is likely weighing down efforts to </span><a class="editor-rtfLink" href="https://www.pymnts.com/apple/2025/apples-siri-once-a-pioneer-now-an-ai-laggard/" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><span data-preserver-spaces="true">introduce an upgraded Siri</span></a><span data-preserver-spaces="true">.</span></p>
<p><span data-preserver-spaces="true">“They are falling behind because of this fear of not being perfect,” said Julia.</span></p>
