<h2><strong>The birth of Syntilay</strong></h2>
<p><a href="https://www.syntilay.com" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener"><u>Syntilay</u></a>, whose name is derived from &#8220;scintillate&#8221; (meaning to sparkle), is the brainchild of 25-year-old entrepreneur Ben Weiss. The company&#8217;s journey from concept to creation is as unique as the shoes themselves. Using Midjourney AI, they generated the basic shape, which was then sketched by an artist. This sketch was transformed into a 3D model using Vizcom AI, and finally, generative AI added the distinctive patterns and textures. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.foxnews.com/tech/what-is-ai-artificial-intelligence" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><strong>WHAT IS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI)?</strong></a></p>
<p>Ben tells CyberGuy, &#8220;<a href="https://www.foxnews.com/tech" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI designing shoes</a> is going to disrupt the category because of the incredible speed to go to market. New releases for traditionally made shoes take around 18 months for a model to release, with AI making them and 3D printing, that is now only a few months.&#8221;</p>

<h2><strong>Customization meets comfort</strong></h2>
<p>One of Syntilay&#8217;s standout features is its commitment to personalized comfort. Before you can slip your feet into these futuristic slides, you&#8217;ll need to scan your feet <a href="https://www.foxnews.com/category/tech/technologies/smartphones" target="_blank" rel="noopener">using your smartphone</a>. This ensures a custom fit for each customer, even accommodating those with different-sized feet. Available in five colors – blue, black, red, beige and orange – these shoes are priced at a not-so-modest $149.99 per pair.</p>

<h2><strong>The grand plan</strong></h2>
<p>While Syntilay is pushing the boundaries of shoe design, they&#8217;ve also got some serious old-school cred in their corner. Joe Foster, the co-founder of Reebok, is lending his expertise to the project. With Foster&#8217;s experience in building a $4 billion shoe empire, Syntilay is hoping to make big strides in the footwear market. Syntilay&#8217;s ambitions extend far beyond selling a few thousand pairs of quirky slides. </p>
<p>They&#8217;re eyeing the $85.8 billion U.S. footwear market, with plans to open their platform to brands and content creators. This could potentially allow influencers and brands to design and sell their own AI-created footwear, tapping into a market that has traditionally been difficult for non-shoe companies to crack.</p>
