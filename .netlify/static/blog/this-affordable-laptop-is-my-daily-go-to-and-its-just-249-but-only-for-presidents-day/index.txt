<h2>Why I like this tablet</h2>
<p>On the surface, you might think it&#8217;s no different than an iPad or any number of Android tablets. But the big differences are the operating system, ChromeOS, and its price. If you&#8217;ve never used <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/best-chromebook/" target="_self">ChromeOS</a>, it&#8217;s sort of a mix between a traditional desktop OS like Windows or MacOS and a mobile OS that your phone uses. The combination makes it easier for me to get work done with a keyboard and trackpad or mouse, but it also uses apps from the Google Play Store, so it has a mobile device feel too. The only hiccup might be if your work relies on software available only for MacOS or Windows. That&#8217;s never been an issue for me, as there&#8217;s typically a web or mobile app I can use in its place.</p>
<h2>    The Duet 11 helps me stay busy during my train commute    </h2>
<p>The Duet&#8217;s size is really nice for my train commute, letting me easily edit docs or respond to emails on my way to work. It has a fantastic front-facing 5-megapixel camera, too, so I can use it for a Zoom chat when necessary, and the camera has a physical privacy shutter to block it out the rest of the time. I especially like the Duet 11&#8217;s zippy performance. It wakes as soon as I drop the keyboard open, and I can quickly tether to my <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/mobile/best-google-pixel-phone/" target="_self">Pixel phone</a> for an instant internet connection. Battery life is strong, too, at up to 11 hours in my use of it.      </p>
<p>Using the Duet on your lap can be a little bit of a balancing act, but it&#8217;s doable. Its compact size makes it ideal for cramped coffee shop tables and other places where a full-size laptop might be awkward. It also weighs only about 2 pounds with all of its accessories.     </p>
<p><strong>Read more:</strong> <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/computing/why-a-chromebook-is-probably-all-you-need/" target="_self">Why a Chromebook Is Probably All You Need</a></p>
<p>Like a lot of people, I unwind on my commute home with some entertainment, and the Duet also shines for that. Pop off the magnetically attached keyboard and ChromeOS switches to a more touch-friendly experience. The tablet supports cloud gaming services such as <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/gaming/xbox-game-pass-what-to-know-about-each-plan-before-you-subscribe/" target="_self">Xbox Game Pass</a> and <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/gaming/nvidia-geforce-now-review-gaming-anywhere-that-makes-sense/" target="_self">Nvidia GeForce Now</a>, which means that with a strong web connection, you can play all kinds of games. I love to play mobile games on it from the Play Store too.     </p>
<h2>    The Duet&#8217;s also an e-reader  </h2>
<p>In addition to being ideal for commuting and cramped spaces, the Duet is also a good e-reader. I use it for reading manga with the <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/viz-media-launches-2-manga-app-so-you-can-read-english-simulpubs/" target="_self">Viz and Shonen Jump apps</a>, and I regularly dip into my library&#8217;s collection of ebooks and magazines via <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/long-drive-for-the-holidays-you-need-this-free-audible-alternative-in-your-life/" target="_self">the Libby app</a>. The device is good for streaming video and music too, though I prefer <a href="https://www.cnet.com/tech/mobile/best-wireless-headphones/" target="_self">Bluetooth headphones</a> over the tablet&#8217;s little speakers.     </p>
<p>While I mainly use it for travel and on my commute, the Duet is equally great around the house. I wouldn&#8217;t recommend the Duet as your only laptop unless you simply don&#8217;t need to do more than basic home office work and can connect to a larger display, keyboard and mouse. The 11-inch touchscreen, while great-looking, is a little too small for all-day, everyday use. But as a secondary device to a larger, more powerful desktop or laptop, it&#8217;s just about perfect.   </p>
