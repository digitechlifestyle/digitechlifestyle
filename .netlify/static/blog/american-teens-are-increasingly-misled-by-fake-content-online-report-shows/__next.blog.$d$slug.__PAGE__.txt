<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6imqx7l000v2co20cz5dagk@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            As AI has made fake content much easier to produce, a growing number of American teenagers say they are being misled by AI-generated photos, videos or other content on the internet, a new study shows.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vni0001356m97orip3v@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            <a href="https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/research-brief-teens-trust-and-technology-in-the-age-of-ai" target="_blank">The study</a>, published Wednesday by Common Sense Media, a nonprofit advocacy group, asked 1,000 teenagers aged 13 to 18 about their experiences with media made by<strong> </strong>generative AI tools. About 35% reported being deceived by fake content online. However, a larger 41% reported they had encountered content that was real yet misleading and 22% said they had shared information that turned out to be fake.<em> </em>
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0002356mghj1wqvv@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            The findings come as a growing number of teenagers adopt artificial intelligence. A <a href="https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-dawn-of-the-ai-era-teens-parents-and-the-adoption-of-generative-ai-at-home-and-school" target="_blank">September Common Sense study</a> showed that seven in 10 teenagers had at least tried generative AI.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0003356m6hhg8d7p@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            Two years after ChatGPT’s launch, the AI arena has grown increasingly crowded, with DeepSeek’s meteoric arrival on Monday. But the top models are still prone to AI hallucinations, according to a <a href="https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2407.17468" target="_blank">July 2024</a> study from Cornell, the University of Washington and the University of Waterloo — meaning even the top AI platforms still create false information out of thin air.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0004356mwjcdnnvm@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            And teenagers who encountered fake online content were more likely to say AI would exacerbate their verification of online information, according to Wednesday’s study from Common Sense, a media and tech nonprofit<em>.</em>
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0005356mgns63d5w@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            The survey also pressed teenagers about their thoughts on major tech corporations, including Google, Apple, Meta, TikTok and Microsoft. Nearly half of teenagers don’t trust Big Tech to make responsible decisions about how they use AI, according to the study.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0006356ms4n7d5pw@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            “The ease and speed at which generative AI allows everyday users to spread unreliable claims and inauthentic media may exacerbate teens’ existing low levels of trust in institutions like the media and government,” Wednesday’s study said.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0007356msxuu48xe@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            Teenagers’ distrust of Big Tech echo a growing dissatisfaction with major tech companies in the United States. American adults also have to contend with increases in misleading or altogether fake content, exacerbated by the erosion of already limited digital guardrails.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0008356m9vrlgq5o@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            Since acquiring Twitter in 2022 and renaming the platform X, Elon Musk has gutted its moderation teams, allowed misinformation and hate speech to spread and reinstated the accounts of previously banned conspiracy theorists, among other moves. Recently, Meta moved to replace third-party fact-checkers with Community Notes, which CEO Mark Zuckerberg has noted will lead to more harmful content appearing across Facebook, Instagram and its other platforms.
    </p>
<p class="paragraph inline-placeholder vossi-paragraph" data-uri="cms.cnn.com/_components/paragraph/instances/cm6jb3vnj0009356m1gpnvgek@published" data-editable="text" data-component-name="paragraph" data-article-gutter="true">
            “Teens’ perceptions of the accuracy of online content signal a distrust in digital platforms, pointing to an opportunity for educational interventions on misinformation for teens,” the study found, adding that there’s also a “need for tech companies to prioritize transparency and develop features that enhance the credibility of the content shared on their platforms.”
    </p>
