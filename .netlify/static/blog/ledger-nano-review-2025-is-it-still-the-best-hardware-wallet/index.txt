<p>If you hold more than £500 in cryptocurrency, you need a hardware wallet. Full stop. Leaving crypto on an exchange means you do not truly own it — exchanges get hacked, go bankrupt, and freeze withdrawals. With a hardware wallet, you are the only person who can access your funds.</p>
<p>Ledger is the most trusted name in hardware wallets. Over 6 million devices have been sold worldwide. After testing both the Nano X and Nano S Plus extensively, here is my honest review.</p>
<h2>Ledger Nano X — Best Overall</h2>
<p>The Nano X is Ledger&apos;s flagship device. It connects to your phone via Bluetooth, supports over 5,500 coins and tokens, and can store up to 100 apps simultaneously. The build quality is excellent — it feels premium and solid in hand.</p>
<p><strong>Key specs:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Supports 5,500+ cryptocurrencies</li>
<li>Bluetooth + USB-C connectivity</li>
<li>Stores up to 100 apps at once</li>
<li>Battery-powered (up to 8 hours)</li>
<li>CC EAL5+ certified secure element chip</li>
</ul>
<h2>Is Ledger Safe?</h2>
<p>Yes. Ledger uses a dedicated Secure Element chip — the same technology used in bank cards and passports. Your private keys never leave the device. Even if your computer is completely compromised by malware, a hacker cannot steal your crypto without physically pressing the buttons on your Ledger.</p>
<p>The only security incident Ledger has ever had was a marketing database breach in 2020 — no funds were ever stolen. The devices themselves have never been compromised.</p>
<h2>Ledger Nano S Plus — Best Value</h2>
<p>If you want Ledger security at a lower price point, the Nano S Plus is the answer. It does not have Bluetooth (USB-C only) but otherwise offers almost identical security to the Nano X. At around £59, it is outstanding value.</p>
<h2>Verdict</h2>
<p>Ledger hardware wallets are the gold standard for a reason. If you are serious about holding crypto long-term, owning a Ledger is not optional — it is essential. The peace of mind alone is worth every penny.</p>
