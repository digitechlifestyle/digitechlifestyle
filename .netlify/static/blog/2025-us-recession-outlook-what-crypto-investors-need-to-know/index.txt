<h2>Understanding Recessions: Definitions and Historical Context</h2>
<p>The line between economic slowdown and full-blown recession is blurrier than most realize. <h3>How Economists Define a Recession</h3>
<p>NBER’s <strong>research</strong>-driven approach examines:  </p>
<ul>
<li><strong>GDP contraction</strong>: Sustained drops in economic output.</li>
<li><strong>Unemployment spikes</strong>: Job losses exceeding 0.5% over three months.</li>
<li><strong>Consumer confidence dips</strong>: Sentiment shifts that curb spending.</li>
</ul>
<p>This multi-angle view explains why the 2020 COVID slump—though brief—counted as a recession, while the 2008 crisis dragged on for <em>years</em>.</p>
<h3>Key Recession Indicators: GDP, Employment, and Consumer Sentiment</h3>
