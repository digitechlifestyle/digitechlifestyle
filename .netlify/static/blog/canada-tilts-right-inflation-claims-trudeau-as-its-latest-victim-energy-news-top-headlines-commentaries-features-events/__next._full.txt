<h2><strong>The prime minister’s resignation marks both the end of a political era and the beginning of a potentially dramatic reshaping of the nation’s economy.</strong></h2>
<p><strong>By <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AS5AQQizNJE/thomas-seal" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Thomas Seal</a>, <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ASMz3hGbX_o/erik-hertzberg" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Erik Hertzberg</a>, <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/ATzepF6tfVw/randy-thanthongknight" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Randy Thanthong-Knight</a>, and <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/authors/AVhsUwCy8eU/brian-platt" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Brian Platt</a></strong>
</p>
<p><img fetchpriority="high" fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" width="1024" height="691" class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-602401" src="https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1-1024x691.jpg" alt="inflation claims trudeau as its latest victim 1200x810" srcset="https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1-1024x691.jpg 1024w, https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1-300x203.jpg 300w, https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1-400x270.jpg 400w, https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1-768x518.jpg 768w, https://energynow.com/wp-content/uploads/Inflation-Claims-Trudeau-as-Its-Latest-Victim-1200x810-1.jpg 1200w" sizes="(max-width: 1024px) 100vw, 1024px"/>
</p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><em>Justin Trudeau, Canada’s prime minister, departs after announcing his resignation at a news conference in Ottawa.Photographer: David Kawai/Bloomberg</em>
</p>
<p>As Justin Trudeau’s resignation throws Canada into political turmoil, at least one thing seems certain: An economy centered on record immigration, expanded government spending and green ambitions is set for a dramatic reshaping.
</p>
<p>The end of the prime minister’s nine-year reign in large part reflects <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-30/canadians-are-growing-pessimistic-about-economy-ahead-of-trump-s-return" target="_blank" rel="noopener">mounting discontent</a> over the cost of living and weak economic growth. Now, as Canada prepares for the Donald Trump era, its Conservative Party is in <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-04/-gutless-canada-s-ceos-are-jolted-by-trudeau-s-surging-rival" target="_blank" rel="noopener">prime position</a> to take power and push the country away from the progressive policies that defined Trudeau’s tenure.
</p>
<p>It’s a shift that has played out around the world as economic angst, fueled by inflation and higher interest rates, topples ruling parties both on the left and right. After expanding Canada’s social safety net — and budget deficits — Trudeau ultimately couldn’t overcome a collapse in popularity and disarray within his administration.
</p>
<p>Inflation “is a government killer, and it doesn’t pay any attention to the ideological bent of the government in power,” said Gerald Butts, a political consultant at Eurasia Group who was principal secretary to Trudeau until 2019. “Like a lot of governments, they were slow to recognize both the fact of inflation and the remedies that were going to be required.”
</p>
<p>While Trudeau’s Liberal Party will now pick Canada’s new leader, the country is likely to face a national election almost immediately afterward. The Liberals are polling more than 20 points behind the Conservatives, according to some surveys, putting taxes, spending and environmental regulations on the chopping block in the world’s fourth-largest oil-producing nation.
</p>
<p>Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre’s signature policy is to eliminate Trudeau’s carbon tax on consumers. He’d also ramp up production of raw materials.
</p>
<p>“You’ll find a more pro-business, pro-resource extraction, smaller government, smaller spending, tougher-on-crime kind of a government” if the Conservatives prevail, said Christopher Hernandez-Roy, deputy director of the Americas program at the Center for Strategic and International Studies in Washington.
</p>
<p>Such policies would more closely resemble those of Trump, who was <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-06/trump-declared-election-winner-in-ceremony-four-years-after-riot?srnd=homepage-americas" target="_blank" rel="noopener">certified</a> as the US presidential election winner just hours after Trudeau’s resignation. He has <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-26/trump-tariff-threat-brings-unease-in-mexico-canada-halls-of-power" target="_blank" rel="noopener">threatened 25% tariffs</a> on his northern neighbor and has <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-18/trump-publicly-taunts-trudeau-saying-canada-should-be-a-us-state" target="_blank" rel="noopener">mocked Canada</a> as the 51st US state.
</p>
<p>The president-elect — who has also posted expansionist rhetoric about Panama and Greenland — repeated the taunt Monday, and reiterated his complaint that the US trade deficit with Canada is somehow a subsidy. The deficit is a consequence of the US buying more than 4 million barrels per day of Canadian oil.
</p>
<p>“The United States can no longer suffer the massive Trade Deficits and Subsidies that Canada needs to stay afloat,” Trump said in a Truth Social post. “Justin Trudeau knew this, and resigned.” Canada’s trade balance with the US was C$91 billion ($63.5 billion) in the first 11 months of the year, according to Statistics Canada data.
</p>
<p>Trump himself was elected in November amid voter discontent with the US economy under President Joe Biden, who was forced to drop his reelection bid and was replaced as a candidate by Vice President Kamala Harris.
</p>
<h3><strong>Power Shift</strong></h3>
<p>Trudeau, 53, will remain as prime minister until a new leader is selected, likely sometime in March. The <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-06/carney-freeland-lead-list-of-contenders-to-succeed-trudeau-as-canada-s-pm" target="_blank" rel="noopener">potential contenders</a> include Chrystia Freeland, whose <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-20/the-call-with-chrystia-freeland-that-threw-justin-trudeau-government-into-crisis" target="_blank" rel="noopener">shock resignation</a> as finance minister last month ultimately led to Trudeau’s demise; Dominic LeBlanc, a Trudeau ally who replaced her; and Mark Carney, the former governor of the Bank of Canada who also serves as chair of Brookfield Asset Management Ltd. and Bloomberg Inc.
</p>
<p>Carney said late Monday that he’s considering entering the race. Freeland also has allies within the Liberal caucus — and her resignation may have helped her politically. Some 45% of respondents said they had an improved impression of her after that event, according to a Nanos Research Group poll taken for Bloomberg News, while 69% said their impression of Trudeau worsened as a result.
</p>
<p>But the Liberals will then face another national election that could come as soon as April, and must happen by late October. Barring a dramatic turnaround, they’d face an uphill battle against Poilievre and the Conservatives.
</p>
<p>Residents have grown increasingly dissatisfied with Trudeau’s economy. Canada’s per-capita gross domestic product contracted for six straight quarters and fell 3.5% since its peak in 2022, a never-before-seen decline outside of a recession. There aren’t enough jobs for everybody who wants one and unemployment <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-06/canada-jobless-rate-jumps-to-6-8-raising-odds-of-big-cut?sref=5GEyPfDL" target="_blank" rel="noopener">keeps rising</a>. Wages can’t keep up with surges in <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-25/food-inflation-in-canada-outpaces-wage-gains-fuels-worker-angst" target="_blank" rel="noopener">grocery prices</a>. The dream of homeownership appears further out of reach for many young Canadians, a key voting bloc that helped elect Trudeau.
</p>
<p>Conservatives have swooped in on these grievances. They blame Trudeau’s mismanagement of immigration, taxes on carbon emissions and government spending for fueling inflation and making lives more expensive, and vow to reverse course.
</p>
<p>“Canadians deserve a real choice in the next election and it has become obvious to me, with the internal battles, that I cannot be the one to carry the Liberal standard into the next election,” Trudeau said Monday.
</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>Even if Trudeau’s replacement as Liberal leader seeks to run on a platform of change, “it’s an academic exercise,” said John Ruffolo, the founder of Toronto-based Maverix Private Equity. “Whoever is running the party, I believe, is going to lose in a landslide win for the Conservatives. So it doesn’t really matter at the end of the day.”
</p>
<p>Poilievre, 45, has tried to position himself as a strong man and cast Trudeau as a weak leader. He has blamed the prime minister for losing control of the <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-12-17/canada-expands-aerial-border-surveillance-to-pacify-trump" target="_blank" rel="noopener">border</a>, an issue Trump has raised as he tries to curb illegal drugs and migrants from Canada and Mexico to the US.
</p>
<p>While Trudeau’s government had the experience of negotiating with Trump during his first administration, Canadians have viewed Poilievre as equal to the prime minister in managing the country’s relationship with the incoming president. A poll by Nanos Research for Bloomberg News, done in late November and early December, asked which leader was best to handle Trump; 36% said Poilievre and 34% said Trudeau.</p>
<p>Poilievre posted a video on Monday set to brooding electronic background music declaring that Trudeau and his colleagues in the Liberal Party helped to “break the country.”
</p>
<p>“Canadians can take back control of their lives and their country, take back control of our border, take back control of immigration, take back control of spending, deficits and inflation, take back control of our streets,” and put “Canada first,” he said.
</p>

</p>
<h3><strong>Fiscal Issues</strong></h3>
<p>Taxes and government spending emerged as a key issue for Trudeau. In her resignation letter, Freeland highlighted her disagreements with the prime minister on fiscal policy, suggesting that proposed <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-21/trudeau-to-unveil-tax-relief-in-deal-with-left-wing-opposition" target="_blank" rel="noopener">tax rebate checks</a> were irresponsible political gimmicks.
</p>
<p>The country’s federal debt has nearly doubled since 2015, rising to C$1.24 trillion ($870 billion) in fiscal 2023-24 — mostly due to generous support payments sent to businesses and households during the Covid-19 pandemic. But the federal government has also added billions in permanent new spending measures, including child benefit checks, subsidized daycare and a national dental plan. Total program expenses were 16.2% of the economy last fiscal year, the highest proportion since the early 1990s, outside of the Covid-19 pandemic.
</p>
<p>“Various potential outcomes after Trudeau — whether a different Liberal prime minister or another party in power — suggest tighter fiscal policy going forward,” said Dominique Lapointe, an economist with Manulife Investment Management.
</p>

</p>

</p>
<p>Some of Trudeau’s signature expansions of the social safety net may be harder to roll back, such as dental and pharmaceutical care, said Laura Stephenson, a political science professor at Western University in London, Ontario. Beyond cutting taxes and costs, private equity founder Ruffolo said that Conservatives would likely focus on aligning with the US on areas like energy, critical minerals and defense.
</p>
<p>“It’s obviously bullish for the oil and gas sector,” said Heather Exner-Pirot, director of energy, natural resources and environment at the Macdonald-Laurier Institute think tank, pointing to potential reform or removal of Canada’s proposed <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-11-04/trudeau-to-order-oil-gas-producers-to-cut-emissions-by-35" target="_blank" rel="noopener">emissions cap</a>, its laws around environmental impacts, clean fuel regulations and electric vehicle sales mandate.
</p>
<h3><strong>Crisis of Confidence</strong></h3>
<p>In the end, Trudeau’s administration was undone by a broader crisis of confidence. His own party lost faith in his ability to win over the public with a clear message and agenda. The country lost confidence in the government’s ability to take action to ease economic pain.
</p>
<p>Trudeau’s political brand was also damaged by the government’s handling of the immigration file. His government steadily ramped up immigration targets, seeing it as <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2023-06-18/canada-s-immigration-policies-may-boost-its-labor-market-economy" target="_blank" rel="noopener">tool for boosting economic growth</a>. After the Covid pandemic eased, the country saw massive increases in international students, temporary workers and other new arrivals that pushed population growth to the <a href="https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-27/population-gains-in-canada-set-record-but-slower-growth-ahead" target="_blank" rel="noopener">highest levels on record</a> — a faster pace than any other developed nation.
</p>
<p>It overwhelmed the country’s capacity to absorb so many new people. Housing shortages worsened and rent prices soared, and public support for immigration plunged. The episode seemed to underscore that even when the government’s intentions were good, it had difficulty getting the policy details right.
</p>
<p>All of that happened before Trump won in November.
</p>
<p>Since then, consumer sentiment has been slipping — about 60% of Canadians believe the economy is about to get worse. Business investment is in a funk. The arrival of the new US administration, with tariffs, tax cuts and an America-first mindset, could worsen that, at least in the short term.
</p>
<p>It may be up to Poilievre, should he win the general election, to handle the fallout from Trump’s policies. A Conservative government led by him is likely to focus primarily on economic matters, while pushing back on the brand of cultural progressivism for which Trudeau was a standard-bearer. Some of Trudeau’s signature policies around equality were memorable — such as trying to address Canada’s legacy of injustice against Indigenous people, or appointing a cabinet with equal numbers of women and men. But sometimes the prime minister also alienated voters.
</p>
<p>Poilievre and his allies dismiss much of Trudeau’s liberal politics as “woke” and “wacko.”
</p>
<p>Despite the widespread discontent with the government, the political shift in Canada is more reflective of a need for change after almost a decade of leadership by one party than a dramatic tilt, said Stephenson, the Western University professor.
</p>
<p>“It’s not necessarily a right wing turn in Canada, so much as it’s time for change,” she said. “The only alternative is the other party that’s ever held office, and that’s where they’re going.”
</p>
