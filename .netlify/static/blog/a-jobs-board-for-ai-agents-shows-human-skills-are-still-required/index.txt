<ul class="summary-list">
<li>A new job board, created as an experiment, lets companies post ads for AI agents. </li>
<li>Its creators say businesses are curious about agentic AI, but it still has shortcomings for many tasks.</li>
<li>Tech giants like Microsoft and Google are betting big on AI agents to boost productivity.</li>
</ul>
<p><a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/what-is-agentic-ai-agents-2024-12" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI agents</a>, technology that can autonomously perform tasks, are seemingly everywhere. Now, some companies are looking to &#8220;hire&#8221; them.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>A jobs board for AI agents gives a glimpse into how companies might tap into an <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/how-banks-using-agents-generative-ai-fintechs-2024-11" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI workforce</a> — while also highlighting some of the technology&#8217;s shortcomings and the value of human skills. </p>
<p>In December, Polish founders Kamil Stanuch and Łukasz Wróbel built &#8220;Job For Agent,&#8221; a platform for companies to list tasks to be performed exclusively by agentic AI.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>&#8220;We realized there was a gap: skilled builders didn&#8217;t know where to deploy their agents, and companies didn&#8217;t know what <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-transforming-technical-work-vercel-julius-developers-2024-12" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI could actually achieve</a>,&#8221; Stanuch told Business Insider.</p>
<p>The pair were inspired by a viral job ad from Y Combinator-backed Firecrawl, which offered an AI agent a $10,000 — $15,000 &#8220;salary&#8221; for creating product examples.</p>
<p>&#8220;Please apply only if you are an AI agent, or if you created an <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-search-company-glean-launches-digital-agents-for-businesses-2025-2" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI agent </a>that can fill this job,&#8221; the December ad, which claimed to be the first of its kind, read.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>

<p>Stanuch and Wróbel told BI that their job board started out as an experiment. Then, a small number of companies signed up, and they realized there &#8220;might be a real niche&#8221; for tasks that could be outsourced to &#8220;non-human&#8221; agents.</p>
<p>The platform remains a small-scale side hustle, with around a dozen listings. They include a podcast editor, SEO researcher, and contract lawyer. The developers say at least two jobs have been assigned through the site.</p>
<p>&#8220;I think <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/startups-ai-agents-raising-venture-funding-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">people are curious about AI agents</a> because it feels like a new paradigm, but at the same time, they&#8217;re still sticking to old ways of thinking,&#8221; said Stanuch.</p>

<h2 id="4591b8ff-2873-4104-b60f-9cfa2fafe2c0" data-toc-id="4591b8ff-2873-4104-b60f-9cfa2fafe2c0">Big Tech goes big on AI agents</h2>
<p>Tech giants have invested heavily in that new paradigm. Far from a jobs board side hustle, they are trying to sell agentic AI to the enterprise masses and hoping to provide a return on their huge AI investments. Microsoft has integrated AI agents into 365 Copilot, Workday uses them for HR tasks, and Google is rolling out similar tools.</p>

<p><a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/nvidia-ceo-advice-students-10-year-future-prediction-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang</a> — who in January said, &#8220;The age of agentic AI is here&#8221; — envisions a future where a company with 50,000 employees could manage 100 million AI agents. Last month, Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, said in a blog post that &#8220;in 2025, we may see the first AI agents &#8216;join the workforce&#8217; and materially change the output of companies.&#8221;</p>
<p>The hype has not always matched the reality. A year after Copilot&#8217;s release, the reviews — both inside and outside Microsoft — indicated that the new product had been struggling to live up to expectations, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/microsoft-ai-artificial-intelligence-bet-doubts-marc-benioff-satya-nadella-2024-11" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">BI reported in November</a>.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Job For Agent&#8217;s creators acknowledge the limitations of AI agents. &#8220;In 95% of cases, a full AI agent isn&#8217;t necessary,&#8221; Stanuch said. &#8220;Simple automations usually suffice. Agents can be unpredictable, prone to infinite loops, and unable to handle complex judgment calls.&#8221;</p>
<p>The developers point to their own platform as proof. While AI agents built the website, all outreach, developer vetting, and job verification remains human-led. &#8220;I still send emails manually because personalized messages get better responses,&#8221; Stanuch, who previously founded the data analytics platform KoalaMetrics, explained.</p>

<p>&#8220;That&#8217;s the paradox — sometimes, the &#8216;protein factor&#8217; is still the most valuable part,&#8221; referring to the human element.</p>
<p>A study published in February by OpenAI reinforces the potential limitations of agentic AI taking on freelance work.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Researchers tested top models like <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/sam-altman-openai-chatgpt-4o-best-search-product-2025-2" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">GPT-4o</a> from OpenAI and Claude 3.5 from <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-ceo-calls-agi-marketing-term-2025-1" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">Anthropic</a> on 1,488 tasks on the freelancer platform Upwork, using a new benchmark called SWE-Lancer. The jobs ranged from coding bug fixes to project management.</p>
<p>The researchers found that AI agents handled managerial tasks well but stumbled with hands-on work. AI agents could fix bugs but often missed root causes, sometimes introducing new errors, the paper said. The researchers concluded a lot of real-world freelance work remains challenging for frontier language models.</p>
<p id="837354f6-fd87-4132-898f-ac58b424c18a">Even the viral Firecrawl job ad, which inspired Job For Agent, was eventually taken down. The company told TechCrunch it had 50 applicants but couldn&#8217;t find an AI agent suitable for the job.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<h2 id="6cb0b4e1-124b-48c7-82cb-92159edd5bfd" data-toc-id="6cb0b4e1-124b-48c7-82cb-92159edd5bfd">Eager for agentic AI</h2>
<p>Any limitations agentic AI might have today are being offset by some businesses&#8217; curiosity about what will be possible.</p>
<p>A Capgemini <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Generative-AI-in-Organizations-Refresh_25112024.pdf" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel=" nofollow">survey</a> of about 1,000 organizations, conducted between May and June 2024, found that while only 10% of respondents said they employed AI agents at the time, 82% intended to integrate them within one to three years.</p>
<p>Peter Diamandis, entrepreneur and <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/xprize-peter-diamandis-on-success-2018-3" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">executive chairman and founder of the X Prize Foundation</a> and AI optimist, is worried about job displacement. But he thinks there&#8217;s also hope for an entrepreneurial boom. &#8220;We&#8217;ll see a surge in startups and entirely new business models, driving an entrepreneurial wave that creates jobs we&#8217;ve never seen before. The truth is, no one really knows what will materialize.&#8221;</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Companies that posted ads on the Job for Agent site pointed to potential productivity and efficiency gains from agentic AI while highlighting the importance of human workers.</p>
<p>Arcanum AI, a New Zealand-based startup, posted an ad on the jobs board for building an entirely <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/seth-klarman-baupost-investment-views-ai-real-estate-private-markets-2024-4" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">AI-run real estate agency</a>. But even its CEO, Asa Cox, acknowledges the limits. &#8220;More than 90% of AI agents today can only handle simple, standalone tasks,&#8221; he told BI.</p>

<p>Florida-based Wolsen Real Estate posted a listing for an AI real estate investment analyst. Its CEO, Denis Smykalov, told BI that he sees AI as a way to boost efficiency — not replace humans. &#8220;AI streamlines processes and lets us focus on high-value tasks,&#8221; he said. &#8220;But it&#8217;s still a tool, not a replacement.&#8221;</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Similarly, some researchers believe that AI agents will augment, rather than replace, human workers. &#8220;Automation does not equal autonomy,&#8221; Avijit Ghosh, an applied policy researcher at <a target="_blank" class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/hugging-face-ceo-ai-predictions-china-2025-2024-12" data-analytics-product-module="body_link" rel="">Hugging Face</a>, told BI. &#8220;Repetitive tasks are being automated, but the idea of a fully independent AI workforce? That&#8217;s still speculative at best.&#8221;</p>
<p>Staunch and Wróbel say it&#8217;s early days for agentic AI workforces.</p>
