2:T1978,<p>Most people hear about Bitcoin first. Then they hear about Ethereum and wonder: is it just another Bitcoin? The answer is no — Ethereum is something different and, in many ways, more interesting.</p>
<div style="background:#f0f4ff;border:1.5px solid #1a2744;border-radius:10px;padding:22px 26px;margin:24px 0 28px;font-family:sans-serif"><span style="font-size:.68rem;font-weight:700;letter-spacing:.12em;text-transform:uppercase;color:#1a2744;display:block;margin-bottom:6px">One-Line Summary</span><span style="font-size:.95rem;color:#2d3748;display:block;line-height:1.6">Bitcoin is digital money. Ethereum is a programmable network that runs decentralised applications — and has its own digital currency (ETH) that powers it.</span></div>
<h2>What Is Ethereum, Exactly?</h2>
<p>Ethereum is a global, decentralised computing network. You can think of it as a massive shared computer — one that nobody owns and everyone can use. It runs 24/7, never goes offline, and nobody can shut it down.</p>
<p>That shared computer runs programs called <strong>smart contracts</strong> — self-executing agreements written in code. When conditions are met, the contract executes automatically, without any bank, lawyer, or middleman.</p>
<p>ETH (or Ether) is the currency that pays for computation on the Ethereum network. When you use an app built on Ethereum, you pay a small fee in ETH.</p>
<h2>Bitcoin vs Ethereum: The Core Difference</h2>
<p>Bitcoin was designed to do one thing: transfer value without a bank. It does that extremely well. Ethereum was designed to do everything else — run applications, create tokens, power decentralised finance, NFTs, and more.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bitcoin:</strong> Digital gold. Store of value. 21 million coin limit. Simple, secure, conservative.</li>
<li><strong>Ethereum:</strong> Programmable money. Platform for apps. Used for DeFi, NFTs, stablecoins, gaming.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most serious crypto investors hold both. Bitcoin is the safe, long-term store of value. Ethereum is the bet on crypto infrastructure.</p>
<h2>What Is Ethereum Actually Used For?</h2>
<p>Ethereum powers most of the interesting things happening in crypto:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>DeFi (Decentralised Finance):</strong> Borrow, lend, and earn interest without a bank. Protocols like Aave and Uniswap run on Ethereum.</li>
<li><strong>Stablecoins:</strong> USDC and DAI are both Ethereum tokens. They let you hold a dollar-pegged asset without using a bank.</li>
<li><strong>NFTs:</strong> Most NFT collections were issued as Ethereum tokens (ERC-721 standard).</li>
<li><strong>DAOs:</strong> Decentralised Autonomous Organisations use Ethereum smart contracts for governance.</li>
<li><strong>Layer 2 networks:</strong> Polygon, Arbitrum, and Optimism are built on top of Ethereum to make it faster and cheaper.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How Does Ethereum Work?</h2>
<p>Ethereum runs on a blockchain — a database that thousands of computers around the world maintain simultaneously. No single entity controls it.</p>
<p>In 2022, Ethereum switched from &#8220;Proof of Work&#8221; (mining) to &#8220;Proof of Stake&#8221; (The Merge). This reduced Ethereum&#8217;s energy consumption by 99.95% and allowed ETH holders to &#8220;stake&#8221; their ETH to earn rewards — currently around 4–5% annually.</p>
<h2>Ethereum&#8217;s Price History</h2>
<p>ETH launched at $0.31 in 2015. It hit $4,878 at its 2021 peak. In 2025, ETH trades between $2,000–$4,000 depending on broader market conditions. It remains the second-largest cryptocurrency by market cap, behind Bitcoin.</p>
