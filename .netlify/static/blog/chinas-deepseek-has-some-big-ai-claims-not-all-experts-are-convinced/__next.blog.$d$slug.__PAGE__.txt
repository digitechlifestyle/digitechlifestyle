<div id="RegularArticle-ArticleBody-5" data-module="ArticleBody" data-test="articleBody-2" data-analytics="RegularArticle-articleBody-5-2"><span class="HighlightShare-hidden" style="top:0;left:0"/></p>
<div class="group">
<p>Chinese artificial intelligence firm DeepSeek <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/27/nvidia-falls-10percent-in-premarket-trading-as-chinas-deepseek-triggers-global-tech-sell-off.html">rocked markets</a> this week with claims its new AI model outperforms OpenAI&#8217;s and cost a fraction of the price to build.</p>
<p>The assertions — specifically that DeepSeek&#8217;s large language model cost just $5.6 million to train — have sparked concerns over the eyewatering sums that tech giants are currently spending on computing infrastructure required to train and run advanced AI workloads.</p>
<p>Investor fears over DeepSeek&#8217;s disruptive impact <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/27/nvidia-sheds-almost-600-billion-in-market-cap-biggest-drop-ever.html">erased close to $600 billion from Nvidia&#8217;s market capitalization</a> Monday — the biggest single-day drop for any company in U.S. history.</p>
<p>But not everyone is convinced by DeepSeek&#8217;s claims.</p>
<p>CNBC asked industry experts for their views on DeepSeek, and how it actually compares to OpenAI, creator of viral chatbot ChatGPT which sparked the AI revolution.</p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline0"/>What is DeepSeek?</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>Last week, DeepSeek released R1, its <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/27/chinas-deepseek-ai-tops-chatgpt-app-store-what-you-should-know.html">new reasoning model</a> that rivals OpenAI&#8217;s o1. A reasoning model is a large language model that breaks prompts down into smaller pieces and considers multiple approaches before generating a response. It is designed to process complex problems in a similar way to humans.</p>
<p>DeepSeek was founded in 2023 by Liang Wenfeng, co-founder of AI-focused quantitative hedge fund High-Flyer, to focus on large language models and reaching artificial general intelligence, or AGI.</p>
<p>AGI as a concept loosely refers to the idea of an AI that equals or surpasses human intellect on a wide range of tasks.</p>
</div>
<div class="group">
<p>Much of the technology behind R1 isn&#8217;t new. What is notable, however, is that DeepSeek is the first to deploy it in a high-performing AI model with — according to the company — considerable reductions in power requirements.</p>
<p>&#8220;The takeaway is that there are many possibilities to develop this industry. The high-end chip/capital intensive way is one technological approach,&#8221; said Xiaomeng Lu, director of Eurasia Group&#8217;s geo-technology practice.</p>
<p>&#8220;But DeepSeek proves we are still in the nascent stage of AI development and the path established by OpenAI may not be the only route to highly capable AI.&#8221; </p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline1"/>How is it different from OpenAI?</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>DeepSeek has two main systems that have garnered buzz from the AI community: V3, the large language model that unpins its products, and R1, its reasoning model.</p>
<p>Both models are open-source, meaning their underlying code is free and publicly available for other developers to customize and redistribute.</p>
<p>DeepSeek&#8217;s models are much smaller than many other large language models. V3 has a total of 671 billion parameters, or variables that the model learns during training. And while OpenAI doesn&#8217;t disclose parameters, experts estimate its latest model to have at least a trillion.</p>
<p>In terms of performance, DeepSeek <a href="https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-R1" target="_blank">says</a> its R1 model achieves performance comparable to OpenAI&#8217;s o1 on reasoning tasks, citing benchmarks including AIME 2024, Codeforces, GPQA Diamond, MATH-500, MMLU and SWE-bench Verified.</p>
</div>
<div class="group">
<div class="RelatedContent-relatedContent" id="RegularArticle-RelatedContent-1">
<div class="RelatedContent-container">

<div class="group">
<p>In a technical report, the company said its V3 model had a training cost of only $5.6 million — a fraction of the billions of dollars that notable Western AI labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic have spent to train and run their foundational AI models. It isn&#8217;t yet clear how much DeepSeek costs to run, however.</p>
<p>If the training costs are accurate, though, it means the model was developed at a fraction of the cost of rival models by OpenAI, Anthropic, <span class="QuoteInBody-quoteNameContainer" data-test="QuoteInBody" id="RegularArticle-QuoteInBody-5"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/GOOGL/">Google</a><span class="QuoteInBody-inlineButton"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistContainer" id="-WatchlistDropdown" data-analytics-id="-WatchlistDropdown"><button class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistButton" aria-label="Add To Watchlist" data-testid="dropdown-btn"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-addWatchListFromTag"/></button></span></span></span> and others.</p>
<p>Daniel Newman, CEO of tech insight firm The Futurum Group, said these developments suggest &#8220;a massive breakthrough,&#8221; although he shed some doubt on the exact figures.</p>
<p>&#8220;I believe the breakthroughs of DeepSeek indicate a meaningful inflection for scaling laws and are a real necessity,&#8221; he said. &#8220;Having said that, there are still a lot of questions and uncertainties around the full picture of costs as it pertains to the development of DeepSeek.&#8221;</p>
<p>Meanwhile, Paul Triolio, senior VP for China and technology policy lead at advisory firm DGA Group, noted it was difficult to draw a direct comparison between DeepSeek&#8217;s model cost and that of major U.S. developers.</p>
<p>&#8220;The 5.6 million figure for DeepSeek V3 was just for one training run, and the company stressed that this did not represent the overall cost of R&amp;D to develop the model,&#8221; he said. &#8220;The overall cost then was likely significantly higher, but still lower than the amount spent by major US AI companies.&#8221; </p>
<p>DeepSeek wasn&#8217;t immediately available for comment when contacted by CNBC.</p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline2"/>Comparing DeepSeek, OpenAI on price</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>DeepSeek and OpenAI both disclose pricing for their models&#8217; computations on their websites.</p>
<p>DeepSeek says R1 costs 55 cents per 1 million tokens of inputs — &#8220;tokens&#8221; referring to each individual unit of text processed by the model — and $2.19 per 1 million tokens of output.</p>
<p>In comparison, OpenAI&#8217;s pricing page for o1 shows the firm charges $15 per 1 million input tokens and $60 per 1 million output tokens. For GPT-4o mini, OpenAI&#8217;s smaller, low-cost language model, the firm charges 15 cents per 1 million input tokens.</p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline3"/>Skepticism over chips</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>DeepSeek&#8217;s reveal of R1 has already led to heated public debate over the veracity of its claim — not least because its models were built despite export controls from the U.S. restricting the use of advanced AI chips to China. </p>
<p>DeepSeek claims it had its breakthrough using mature Nvidia clips, including H800 and A100 chips, which are less advanced than the chipmaker&#8217;s cutting-edge H100s, which can&#8217;t be exported to China.</p>
<p>However, in <a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/23/scale-ai-ceo-says-china-has-quickly-caught-the-us-with-deepseek.html">comments to CNBC last week</a>, Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang, said he believed DeepSeek used the banned chips — a claim that DeepSeek denies.</p>
</div>

<p>Nvidia has since come out and said that the GPUs that DeepSeek used were fully export-compliant.</p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline4"/>The real deal or not?</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>Industry experts seem to broadly agree that what DeepSeek has achieved is impressive, although some have urged skepticism over some of the Chinese company&#8217;s claims.</p>
<p>&#8220;DeepSeek is legitimately impressive, but the level of hysteria is an indictment of so many,&#8221; U.S. entrepreneur Palmer Luckey, who founded Oculus and Anduril wrote on X.</p>
<p>&#8220;The $5M number is bogus. It is pushed by a Chinese hedge fund to slow investment in American AI startups, service their own shorts against American titans like Nvidia, and hide sanction evasion.&#8221;</p>
<p>Seena Rejal, chief commercial officer of NetMind, a London-headquartered startup that offers access to DeepSeek&#8217;s AI models via a distributed GPU network, said he saw no reason not to believe DeepSeek.</p>
<p>&#8220;Even if it&#8217;s off by a certain factor, it still is coming in as greatly efficient,&#8221; Rejal told CNBC in a phone interview earlier this week. &#8220;The logic of what they&#8217;ve explained is very sensible.&#8221;</p>
<p>However, some have claimed DeepSeek&#8217;s technology might not have been built from scratch.</p>
<p>&#8220;DeepSeek makes the same mistakes O1 makes, a strong indication the technology was ripped off,&#8221; billionaire investor Vinod Khosla said on X, without giving more details.</p>
<p>It&#8217;s a claim that OpenAI itself has alluded to, telling CNBC in a statement Wednesday that it is reviewing reports DeepSeek may have &#8220;inappropriately&#8221; used output data from its models to develop their AI model, a method referred to as &#8220;distillation.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;We take aggressive, proactive countermeasures to protect our technology and will continue working closely with the U.S. government to protect the most capable models being built here,&#8221; an OpenAI spokesperson told CNBC.</p>
</div>
<h2 class="ArticleBody-subtitle"><a id="headline5"/>Commoditization of AI</h2>
<div class="group">
<p>However the scrutiny surrounding DeepSeek shakes out, AI scientists broadly agree it marks a positive step for the industry.</p>
<p>Yann LeCun, chief AI scientist at <span class="QuoteInBody-quoteNameContainer" data-test="QuoteInBody" id="RegularArticle-QuoteInBody-7"><a href="https://www.cnbc.com/quotes/META/">Meta</a><span class="QuoteInBody-inlineButton"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistContainer" id="-WatchlistDropdown" data-analytics-id="-WatchlistDropdown"><button class="AddToWatchlistButton-watchlistButton" aria-label="Add To Watchlist" data-testid="dropdown-btn"><span class="AddToWatchlistButton-addWatchListFromTag"/></button></span></span></span>, said that DeepSeek&#8217;s success represented a victory for open-source AI models, not necessarily a win for China over the U.S. Meta is behind a popular open-source AI model called Llama.</p>
<p>&#8220;To people who see the performance of DeepSeek and think: &#8216;China is surpassing the US in AI.&#8217; You are reading this wrong. The correct reading is: &#8216;Open source models are surpassing proprietary ones&#8217;,&#8221; he said in a post on LinkedIn.</p>
<p>&#8220;DeepSeek has profited from open research and open source (e.g. PyTorch and Llama from Meta). They came up with new ideas and built them on top of other people&#8217;s work. Because their work is published and open source, everyone can profit from it. That is the power of open research and open source.&#8221;</p>
