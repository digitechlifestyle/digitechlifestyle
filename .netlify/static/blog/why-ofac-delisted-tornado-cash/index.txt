<p>Last month, the U.S. Treasury Department&#8217;s Office of Foreign Asset Control delisted Tornado Cash from its sanctions list, months after an appeals court ruled that the watchdog could not designate the mixer&#8217;s smart contracts.</p>
<p><em>You’re reading State of Crypto, a CoinDesk newsletter looking at the intersection of cryptocurrency and government. <a href="https://www.coindesk.com/newsletters/state-of-crypto">Click here</a> to sign up for future editions.</em></p>
<h4 class="font-headline-xs font-medium">The narrative</h4>
<p>In November 2024, a Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals panel ruled that the Treasury Department&#8217;s Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) couldn&#8217;t sanction smart contracts tied to crypto mixer Tornado Cash. Last month, OFAC delisted Tornado Cash entirely, though it left developer Roman Semenov on its Specially Designated Nationals list.</p>
<h4 class="font-headline-xs font-medium">Why it matters</h4>
<p>Whether Tornado Cash could be sanctioned to begin with has been a point of contention for the crypto industry. The Fifth Circuit ruling sparked a rally in the TORN token&#8217;s price and raised hopes that it would be more difficult for the U.S. government to block legal uses of mixers.</p>
<h4 class="font-headline-xs font-medium">Breaking it down</h4>
<p><a href="https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/03/21/u-s-government-removes-tornado-cash-sanctions">Tornado Cash&#8217;s delisting</a> included smart contract addresses and other components of the overall mixer, and followed <a href="https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2024/11/26/tornado-cash-sanctions-overturned-by-u-s-appeals-court">November&#8217;s ruling</a>. The delisting may have been an effort to preempt a court ruling that would force OFAC to permanently delist Tornado Cash.</p>
<p>Backing up a little: A group of developers sued OFAC after Tornado Cash was first sanctioned with backing from crypto exchange Coinbase. That case, <a href="https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67077726/joseph-van-loon-v-department-of-the-treasury/?filed_after=&amp;filed_before=&amp;entry_gte=&amp;entry_lte=&amp;order_by=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Van Loon v. Treasury</a>, received an initial ruling from a district court judge that was favorable to the Treasury Department. On appeal, however, the Fifth Circuit ruled — somewhat narrowly — that smart contracts were outside the scope of OFAC&#8217;s jurisdiction. The appeals court panel threw the case back down to the district court to sort out next steps.</p>
<p>On March 21, the same day it removed Tornado Cash from its sanctions list, OFAC <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.txwd.1211705/gov.uscourts.txwd.1211705.107.0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">filed a notice</a> telling the court that the removal meant the legal case remedies cot &#8220;the matter is now moot.&#8221;</p>
<p>Peter Van Valkenburgh, the executive director at Coin Center, said the November decision left OFAC with few options.</p>
<p>&#8220;They could have waited for the court to invalidate the sanctions or they could have delisted them themselves, and they delisted themselves,&#8221; he said. &#8220;You can read that two ways. You can read that as &#8216;I want to try and preserve some ability to fight in the future or [make] some other listing,&#8217; [and] that&#8217;s really tough because that Fifth Circuit opinion is really bad for them.&#8221;</p>
<p>The other read for the delisting is OFAC just wanted the matter resolved quickly, he said.</p>
<p>Leah Moushey, an attorney with Miller &amp; Chevalier, said the court may choose to reject OFAC&#8217;s filing because there&#8217;s an open question as to whether Tornado Cash can be redesignated in the future. She pointed to a Supreme Court case with thematic similarities.</p>
<p>The court said in that case, <a href="https://www.scotusblog.com/case-files/cases/federal-bureau-of-investigation-v-fikre/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">FBI v. Fikre</a>, that the U.S. government had not sufficiently proven that just removing an individual from a no-fly list meant he would never be placed back on the list.</p>
<p>OFAC may have to show in this case that Tornado Cash can&#8217;t be designated again.</p>
<p>Another open question for Tornado Cash is whether the delisting has any bearing on the U.S. Department of Justice&#8217;s criminal case against developer Roman Storm. After the Fifth Circuit ruling, Storm&#8217;s attorneys filed a motion asking the judge overseeing the criminal case to dismiss the indictment, but the judge <a href="https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.604938/gov.uscourts.nysd.604938.127.0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">has already ruled</a> that the case should move forward.</p>
<p>&#8220;The judge determined that the scope of the conduct went beyond the interactions with the smart contract,&#8221; Moushey said. The Fifth Circuit ruling did not discuss Tornado Cash as an entity.</p>
<p>Van Valkenburgh noted that OFAC left its sanctions against Semenov in place, and the DOJ will continue to try and argue Storm conspired to violate sanctions.</p>
<p>The Storm case is currently set for trial in July.</p>
