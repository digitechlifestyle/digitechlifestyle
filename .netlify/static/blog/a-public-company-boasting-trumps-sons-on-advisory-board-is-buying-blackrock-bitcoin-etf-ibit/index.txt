<p>An investment firm with ties to U.S. President Donald Trump’s sons, Eric and Donald Trump Jr., is putting some of its excess cash into a spot bitcoin exchange-traded fund (ETF) — an unusual twist on the increasingly popular strategy of holding bitcoin as a corporate reserve.</p>
<p>Dominari Holdings (DOMH), located in the Trump Tower in New York City, made <a href="https://bit.ly/CryptonomyFinance" target="_blank" rel="noopener">headlines</a> last month after the Trump brothers joined its 58-year-old board of advisors and became investors.</p>
<p>In an <a href="https://bit.ly/CryptonomyFinance" target="_blank" rel="noopener">earnings report</a> on Friday, it announced that it would adopt a bitcoin reserve strategy and invest a portion of its cash reserves into BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), the largest spot bitcoin ETF on the market.</p>
<p>According to the report, Dominari has committed $2 million to buy shares of IBIT as of now. The stock has a market cap of about $70 million and has fallen more than 9% on Friday&#8217;s trading.</p>
<p>Most companies that adopt a bitcoin reserve strategy buy the cryptocurrency outright and self-custody it or use a custodian. Dominari is instead gaining exposure through a regulated exchange-traded fund, a move that may appeal to firms looking for easier compliance and cleaner accounting.</p>
<p>The move isn’t surprising, given Donald Trump Jr.’s interest in crypto. The president’s son is involved in many crypto projects and has become an unofficial spokesperson for his father’s enthusiasm.</p>
<p>Just earlier this week, World Liberty Financial (WLFI), the financial protocol backed by President Donald Trump and his family, <a href="https://bit.ly/CryptonomyFinance">pitched its own stablecoin</a> at a crypto event in Washington.</p>
