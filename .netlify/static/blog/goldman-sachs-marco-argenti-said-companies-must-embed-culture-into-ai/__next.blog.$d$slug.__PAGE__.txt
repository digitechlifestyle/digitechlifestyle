<p>Goldman Sachs&#8217; chief information officer said the real challenge of using AI agents in the workplace isn&#8217;t just making them smarter — it&#8217;s teaching them company culture.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p><a class="" href="https://www.businessinsider.com/goldman-sachs-marco-argenti-wall-street-ai-tech-qa-2024-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-analytics-product-module="body_link">Marco Argenti</a> said on an episode of the Goldman Sachs Exchanges podcast published Tuesday that businesses must figure out how to &#8220;inject&#8221; their cultural traits and leadership principles into AI agents, just as they do with human employees.</p>
<p>The tech industry has mostly focused on the &#8220;specialization of expertise,&#8221; he said. But it doesn&#8217;t talk much about the &#8220;enhancement of the cultural aspect of agents,&#8221; which he said is crucial for managing AI in the workplace.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>AI agents — AI that can act and make decisions independently — are a hot topic in tech and the workforce. <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/mark-zuckerberg-jensen-huang-meta-nvidia-stock-wealth-ai-billionaires-2025-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-analytics-product-module="body_link">Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang</a> said in January that &#8220;the age of agentic AI is here&#8221; and <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/jensen-huang-wants-nvidia-to-have-100-million-ai-assistants-2024-10" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-analytics-product-module="body_link">said </a>last year that Nvidia&#8217;s 50,000-person staff could work with 100 million agents. <a href="https://www.businessinsider.com/tech-leaders-bill-gates-amazon-cto-share-2025-predictions-2025-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-analytics-product-module="body_link">OpenAI&#8217;s Sam Altman predicted that AI agents could start entering the workforce this year.</a></p>
<p>AI agents are intended to be digital coworkers or assistants to human workers in fields ranging from healthcare and supply chain management to cybersecurity and customer service.</p>
<p>Argenti warned that without cultural training, these agents risk missing the nuances that define an organization&#8217;s identity.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>Comparing AI agents to new hires, Argenti said they might become more intelligent over time but won&#8217;t automatically become culturally smarter.</p>
<p>Businesses must figure out how to embed their unique identity into AI agents so they can interpret work through the company&#8217;s lens and communicate in its language.</p>
<p>For instance, he said, an AI agent summarizing portfolio analysis in private wealth management at Goldman Sachs needs to apply a &#8220;Goldman lens&#8221; to its answers. That means translating financial content in a way that aligns with the firm&#8217;s approach.</p>
<p><!-- Excluded mobile ad on desktop --></p>
<p>&#8220;Cultures are very peculiar,&#8221; Argenti added. &#8220;They&#8217;re hard to describe, but you see them when you see them.&#8221;</p>
<p>Argenti acknowledged that he does not know what culturally trained agents look like in practice, asking, &#8220;What are the actual leadership principles or tenets that an effective corporate agent should adhere to in order to fit into the culture of an organization?&#8221;</p>
<p>His answer: &#8220;That&#8217;s a very complex problem that I don&#8217;t think has been solved yet.&#8221;</p>
