## 💼 LinkedIn

$11.4 billion lost to crypto crime in 2025. And the US had no specialist team investigating it.

That's the context behind a new bipartisan bill just introduced in Congress — the Federal Cryptocurrency Theft Enforcement and Coordination Act.

The proposal would create a task force inside the Department of Justice, chaired by the Attorney General, pulling together the FBI, DHS, and Treasury. Its focus: blockchain tracing, evidence preservation, asset recovery, and training local police who currently have no idea how to handle crypto theft reports.

Crucially, the bill explicitly rules out new market regulation or new criminal offences. It's enforcement-only — which is exactly why both Republicans and Democrats can sign it.

For UK professionals watching this space: the FCA conducted its first major crypto enforcement raids in April 2026, hitting eight companies. Full authorisation requirements arrive October 2027. The direction of travel is clear on both sides of the Atlantic.

Does stronger US crypto enforcement actually benefit UK victims — or does it just shift stolen funds to jurisdictions with less cooperation?

Big news for crypto investors — especially those who've worried about what happens when your funds get stolen.

The US Congress just introduced a bill to create a dedicated federal task force for crypto theft. It would sit inside the Department of Justice, with the FBI, DHS and Treasury all working together to trace stolen funds, recover assets, and actually coordinate investigations.

Why now? Because the DOJ scrapped its old crypto crime unit last year, right as losses hit $11.4 billion. The bill is a bipartisan attempt to rebuild those capabilities — without adding new regulations or compliance burdens.

For UK holders, it's worth knowing the FCA is tightening things up on this side of the Atlantic too. Eight crypto firms were raided in April 2026, and the full authorisation regime kicks in October 2027.
