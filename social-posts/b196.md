## LinkedIn Post

The UK electricity grid is balanced second by second. Supply must match demand to within a fraction of a per cent, continuously, at national scale.

For decades, that was done with dispatchable thermal generators — coal, gas, nuclear — that could ramp up or down on command. It worked reliably.

Then renewables arrived at scale. Wind generates when the wind blows. Solar peaks at midday in summer. Demand peaks in winter evenings. The grid became a vastly harder optimisation problem — and that's precisely what AI was built to handle.

By 2026, approximately 44 per cent of UK electricity came from renewable sources. National Grid's ESO now uses machine learning to forecast wind and solar output hours ahead, integrating satellite imagery, weather data, and real-time turbine telemetry. Forecast errors have fallen by around 20 per cent over a 24-hour horizon. Every percentage point of improvement reduces the expensive backup generation the grid has to hold in reserve.

The demand side is changing too. 37 million smart meters are installed across UK homes and businesses. Octopus Energy's Agile tariff reprices electricity every 30 minutes in real time — AI determines the price for each slot. EV owners on AI-optimised charging tariffs save £400 to £600 per year compared to standard overnight charging.

At the asset level, Siemens Gamesa adjusts blade pitch per turbine using ML. SSE Renewables identifies gearbox failure signatures weeks before they'd otherwise be visible. Grid-scale batteries — over 6 GW now operational in the UK — are entirely AI-managed, with AI-optimised systems earning 15 to 25 per cent more annual revenue than simple rule-based approaches.

The Climate Change Committee identified AI as "increasingly central" to hitting the 2030 clean power target without unacceptable cost increases. That's not a prediction. It's a description of what's already happening.
