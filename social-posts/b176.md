**Tweet 3:**
How a neural network learns:

1. Input data → layers of artificial neurons
2. Each neuron multiplies inputs by weights, adds bias, applies activation function
3. Output compared to correct answer
4. Error propagated backwards (backpropagation)
5. Weights adjusted slightly
6. Repeat billions of times

That's it. No rules. Just patterns.

## LinkedIn Post

Everyone uses AI tools. Very few understand what's actually inside them.

Here's a plain-English breakdown of neural networks — the technology behind ChatGPT, NHS diagnostic imaging, and the Netflix algorithm:

**Where they came from:**
The idea dates to 1943. But the modern revolution started in 2012, when AlexNet achieved 15.3% error on ImageNet — versus 26.2% for the second-place entry using traditional methods. Researchers immediately realised deep learning had broken the ceiling.

The enabler? GPUs designed for gaming turned out to be perfect for matrix multiplication — the computation at the heart of neural network training.

**How they learn:**
No rules. No instructions. Just data.

Inputs → weighted connections → activation functions → output → compare to correct answer → propagate error backwards → adjust weights → repeat billions of times.

GPT-4 was trained on several trillion tokens of text. Training cost: tens of millions of pounds.

**The types that matter:**
→ **CNN** — images, video, medical imaging
→ **Transformer** — language (the architecture behind every major LLM)
→ **Diffusion model** — DALL-E 3, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion
→ **GAN** — synthetic faces, deepfake generation

**What they can't do:**
Neural networks pattern-match. They don't reason. They can be confidently wrong. They can't explain their decisions. They amplify training data biases.

Impressive fluency ≠ accuracy.

This matters enormously for anyone deploying AI in UK business contexts — especially in finance, HR, and healthcare where the EU AI Act and UK regulators are starting to require explainability.
