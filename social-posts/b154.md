→ An AI agent takes actions in an environment
→ Gets rewarded for good moves, penalised for bad ones
→ Repeats millions of times until it finds the best strategy

## LinkedIn

Most people know what machine learning is. Far fewer know about the branch of AI that's arguably the most powerful: reinforcement learning.

RL is how AlphaGo defeated the world's best Go player in 2016. It's how robots learn to walk. And — less visibly — it's a core part of how ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini were trained to be helpful.

The concept is simple: reward good behaviour, penalise bad behaviour, repeat millions of times. The AI discovers strategies that no human could design manually.

What makes RL special isn't just that it learns — it's that it can discover solutions humans haven't thought of. AlphaZero reached superhuman level in chess, shogi and Go within 24 hours of training, with no human game records. Just trial, error, and reward signals.

The challenge? Designing the reward correctly. Get it wrong and you get systems that game the metric rather than achieve the goal — a real problem as RL is deployed in healthcare, logistics and autonomous vehicles.
