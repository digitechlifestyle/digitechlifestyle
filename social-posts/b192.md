## LinkedIn Post

Crypto derivatives are among the most actively traded financial instruments in the world. Bitcoin futures alone represent billions of pounds in daily notional volume.

Most UK investors have heard of them. Very few understand what they are, why the FCA banned them for retail, or how HMRC treats them.

Here's the concise version:

**What they are:**
Futures are agreements to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a set date. Options give the right (but not obligation) to buy or sell at a set price. Perpetual futures — unique to crypto — have no expiry and use a funding rate to keep price pegged to spot. All can be used for speculation or hedging. All can be leveraged.

**Why leverage matters:**
At 10x leverage, a 10% adverse price move wipes out your entire position. At 100x (available on offshore exchanges), a 1% move triggers liquidation. Bitcoin's 30%+ single-day drop in May 2021 triggered over $8 billion in cascading liquidations, which amplified the decline.

**The FCA ban:**
In January 2021, the FCA banned the sale of crypto derivatives to retail consumers by FCA-authorised firms — citing volatility, complexity, and unreliable valuations. Many UK retail traders access these products through offshore exchanges like Binance, Bybit, and Deribit, which are not FCA-authorised and carry no UK consumer protections. The FTX collapse in 2022 showed exactly what that means in practice.

**HMRC position:**
Gains from most crypto derivatives are subject to Capital Gains Tax. CGT allowance for 2026/27 is £3,000. Basic rate taxpayers pay 18% above that; higher rate 24%. CFDs may be treated as trading income rather than capital gains depending on activity level. All must be reported via Self Assessment. HMRC has data-sharing arrangements with UK exchanges.

Understanding crypto derivatives is valuable even for investors who will never trade them — they explain significant market behaviour, particularly around options expiry dates and funding rate dynamics in perpetual markets.

Crypto futures and options are some of the most widely traded financial instruments in the world — and the FCA banned UK retail investors from accessing them through regulated firms in January 2021.

Here's what they are and what UK investors need to know:

**Futures:** Agreements to buy or sell crypto at a set price on a set future date. Most cash-settle — no Bitcoin changes hands, you just profit or lose on the price difference.

**Options:** Give the right (not obligation) to buy or sell at a set price before a set date. The buyer pays a premium. That premium is the maximum loss. Unlike futures, you can let an option expire worthless.

**Perpetual futures:** Unique to crypto, no expiry date, price tracked via a funding rate. Account for most crypto derivatives trading globally.

**The leverage problem:** At 10x leverage, a 10% wrong move wipes your account. At 100x — available on offshore exchanges — a 1% move triggers full liquidation. Bitcoin's 30%+ crash in May 2021 triggered $8 billion in forced liquidations in 24 hours, making the crash worse.

**The FCA ban:** UK retail investors cannot legally buy crypto derivatives through FCA-regulated firms. Many use offshore exchanges (Binance, Bybit, Deribit). These offer no FSCS protection, no Ombudsman recourse, and no guarantee your assets are safe if the exchange fails.

**HMRC:** Profits still taxable. Capital Gains Tax applies. £3,000 allowance for 2026/27. Self Assessment required.
