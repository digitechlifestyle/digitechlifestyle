## LinkedIn Post

Bitcoin dominance is moving — and most UK crypto investors don't know what to make of it.

Here's what it is and what it actually signals:

**What it is:**
Bitcoin's market cap as a percentage of total crypto market cap. Introduced by CoinMarketCap in 2013, when BTC was essentially 100% of the market. In early 2026 it's trading in the 52-60% range.

**What the movement means:**
Falling dominance typically signals capital rotating from BTC into altcoins — starting with Ethereum and large-caps, then cascading into smaller tokens. Rising dominance signals the reverse: money retreating to Bitcoin.

This is what happened in 2017 (85% → 40% as the ICO boom exploded) and again in 2021 (73% → 40% as DeFi/NFTs attracted capital before TerraLuna's £37bn collapse reversed the flow).

**Why 2026 is structurally different:**
Spot Bitcoin ETFs in the US and UK LSE-listed Bitcoin ETPs brought institutional capital that flows almost exclusively into BTC through regulated products. Institutions don't rotate into altcoins. This has created a floor under Bitcoin dominance that wasn't present in previous cycles.

**What to watch alongside it:**
→ Fear & Greed Index
→ BTC/ETH ratio
→ Exchange net flows (are holders selling or accumulating?)
→ MVRV ratio (where BTC sits in its historical valuation cycle)

**UK tax reminder:**
Every crypto-to-crypto swap is a disposal under HMRC rules. Six rotations in a bull cycle = six separate CGT events to report, even if you never withdrew to GBP. Keep contemporaneous records of every transaction with sterling value at the time.
