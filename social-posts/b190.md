## LinkedIn Post

Voice AI is one of the most widely deployed forms of artificial intelligence in the UK — and most people don't think about it at all.

Here's what's actually happening when you speak to Siri, Alexa, or a banking phone line:

**The technology:**
Modern speech recognition is built on transformer neural networks — the same architecture behind ChatGPT. OpenAI's Whisper, trained on 680,000 hours of audio across 99 languages, achieves word error rates below 5% for standard English. The BBC now subtitles around 80% of live TV using AI speech recognition, up from less than 20% a decade ago.

**The workplace impact:**
NHS clinicians using AI dictation tools like Nuance DAX reduce documentation time by up to 40% in pilot studies. UK courts, legal firms, and financial services companies use automated transcription daily. The 2023 NHS Long Term Workforce Plan specifically cited AI-assisted documentation as a priority.

**The voice biometrics risk:**
HMRC's telephone helpline and several UK banks use voice biometrics for identity verification. The FCA issued guidance in 2024 on synthetic media risks — voice cloning tools can now fake a voice from just three seconds of audio. Banks should (and increasingly do) require PIN fallbacks for high-risk transactions even when voice authentication passes.

**The regulatory picture:**
The FCA's Consumer Duty regulation requires that automated voice systems in financial services meet the same standards as human agents. UK GDPR applies to voice recordings that can identify individuals. The ICO's 2023 guidance on voice AI makes clear that "it's an AI, not a person" is not a data protection defence.

**The accent gap:**
Significantly higher word error rates for regional UK accents aren't just an inconvenience — for a voice system blocking access to financial services, they're a potential Consumer Duty issue under the Equality Act 2010.
