Most crypto volume doesn't happen in spot markets.

It happens in derivatives — futures, options and perpetual contracts.

And most retail traders who use them lose money.

Quick breakdown:

→ Futures: agree to buy/sell at a set price on a future date
→ Options: the right (not obligation) to buy/sell
→ Perpetuals: futures that never expire + a funding rate mechanism

The danger? Leverage.

At 10x leverage, a 10% move against you = 100% loss.

At 125x leverage (offered by some offshore platforms)? A 0.8% move wipes you out.

Research suggests ~80% of leveraged derivatives traders lose money over 6 months.

## LinkedIn

The majority of crypto trading volume isn't people buying Bitcoin or Ethereum. It's people trading derivatives — contracts that track the price without anyone holding the asset.

Understanding derivatives matters even if you never trade them, because they drive a lot of the volatility you see in spot markets. Funding rate dynamics, large options expirations, and cascading liquidations all affect prices for ordinary holders.

I've put together a beginner's guide covering:

• **Futures** — lock in tomorrow's price today, regulated products available via CME and Kraken
• **Options** — pay a known premium for the right (not obligation) to buy or sell
• **Perpetual futures** — the uniquely crypto instrument with no expiry date and a self-correcting funding rate mechanism
• **Leverage** — the arithmetic of why 80% of leveraged traders statistically lose over any six-month period
• **UK regulation** — the FCA has banned crypto derivatives sales to retail consumers; offshore platforms offering them to UK residents may not be doing so legally

The derivatives market is where professional risk management in crypto actually happens. Approaching it without understanding the mechanics is one of the fastest ways to lose capital in this industry.

Worth reading before you touch anything leveraged.
