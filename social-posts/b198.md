## LinkedIn Post

If you're new to Ethereum, gas fees are usually the first unpleasant surprise. You make what seems like a simple transaction and find a cost nobody mentioned when you bought.

Here's how they actually work — and how to reduce them.

**The mechanics:** Gas measures the computational effort required to execute an operation on Ethereum. Simple ETH transfers use 21,000 gas units. Complex DeFi interactions can use hundreds of thousands. Since EIP-1559 (August 2021), fees have two components: a base fee set automatically by the protocol (and burned — permanently removed from supply), and a priority fee that incentivises validators to include your transaction promptly.

**Why they spike:** Ethereum blocks have a target capacity. When demand for block space exceeds that target, the base fee rises automatically to price out lower-priority transactions. During the DeFi summer of 2020 and the NFT peak in 2021, average transactions cost £50 to £70. During the Bored Ape Yacht Club's Otherside land sale in April 2022, users collectively burned millions in failed transaction fees within a single hour.

**What changed:** The Dencun upgrade in March 2024 introduced blob data structures (EIP-4844) that dramatically reduced Layer 2 costs for settling to mainnet. Layer 2 networks — Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, zkSync — batch thousands of transactions and settle them on mainnet collectively. A swap on Arbitrum in 2025 costs under 1 pence. Mainnet mainnet fees during quiet periods now routinely drop below 3 gwei.

**Practical reduction:** Time non-urgent mainnet transactions for Saturday and Sunday in the early hours of UK time. Use Etherscan's gas tracker. For regular DeFi activity, move to Layer 2 — Base has over 4 million monthly active users and fees consistently under 2 pence.

**HMRC:** Gas fees paid on acquisition are deductible as costs. Gas fees paid on disposal are also deductible. DeFi interactions are more complex and may be income events. All must be reported via Self Assessment.

If you've tried using Ethereum for the first time, you've almost certainly encountered gas fees. They can be confusing, unpredictable, and occasionally painful. Here's the plain-English version.

**What gas fees are:**
Gas measures the computational effort required to execute operations on Ethereum. The fee = gas units used × price per unit (measured in gwei, one billionth of ETH). Since August 2021's EIP-1559 upgrade, fees have a base fee (set by the protocol and permanently burned) plus a priority tip to validators.

**Why they spike so dramatically:**
Ethereum blocks have a target capacity. When more users want to transact than there is block space, the base fee rises automatically to price out lower-priority transactions. In May 2021, average transaction costs hit £50 to £70. During the Bored Ape Yacht Club's Otherside sale in April 2022, users burned millions in failed transaction fees in a single hour.

**What changed since then:**
The March 2024 Dencun upgrade (EIP-4844) added blob data structures that cut Layer 2 settlement costs by 80-90%. Layer 2 networks — Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, zkSync — batch thousands of transactions and settle them on Ethereum mainnet collectively. A DeFi swap on Arbitrum in 2025: under 1p. Base alone has over 4 million monthly active users.

**Practical steps:**
- Move regular DeFi activity to Layer 2 (Base, Arbitrum, Optimism)
- For mainnet: transact Saturday/Sunday 11pm-5am UK for lowest fees
- Check Etherscan gas tracker before every transaction
- Gas fees paid on crypto purchases and sales are deductible from Capital Gains Tax per HMRC guidance
