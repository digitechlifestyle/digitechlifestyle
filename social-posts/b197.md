## LinkedIn Post

AI systems learn from data. The most valuable training data — medical records, financial transactions, behavioural logs — is also the most sensitive. Synthetic data is increasingly how organisations resolve that tension.

Synthetic data is artificially generated information that shares the statistical properties of real-world data without containing actual records from real people. The same distributions, correlations, outliers, and patterns — but no entry corresponds to an actual individual.

Under UK data protection law, personal data is information that can identify a living individual, directly or indirectly. Well-generated synthetic data doesn't meet that definition. This has significant practical implications.

UK GDPR requires a lawful basis for every processing activity — including AI model training. Obtaining meaningful consent from millions of data subjects, or mounting a legitimate interest argument at that scale, is neither straightforward nor always possible. Data minimisation requirements add further pressure: if synthetic data achieves the same model quality, using real data is harder to justify.

The NHS, the Bank of England, and the FCA are already using synthetic data for research that would otherwise be practically impossible. Hazy, a London-based startup, sells synthetic generation tools to financial services firms. The ICO published guidance in 2024 — cautiously supportive but clear that synthetic data isn't automatically outside GDPR scope. The key test is whether it could be reverse-engineered to re-identify real individuals.

Limitations matter too: synthetic data propagates the biases present in the original data, suffers from distribution shift as the real world evolves, and degrades in quality for complex multi-modal records. These are not reasons to avoid it — they're reasons to understand it.

For UK organisations developing AI systems where real data access is restricted by law, contract, or ethics, synthetic data is increasingly the default starting point.

## Facebook Post

AI systems learn from data — and the most valuable training data is usually the most sensitive. Synthetic data is how organisations build AI without handling real personal information.

**What it is:** Artificially generated data with the same statistical properties as real-world data — same distributions, correlations, and patterns — but no actual personal records. Under UK GDPR, well-generated synthetic data likely isn't personal data at all.

**How it's created:**
- GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks) — best for images, medical scans
- Variational Autoencoders — best for tabular data like banking records
- Statistical models — more interpretable, work with smaller datasets

The ONS has used statistical synthetic data for protected census research since 2021.

**Where it's being used in the UK:**
- NHS England — Federated Data Platform uses synthetic generation as a core privacy mechanism
- Bank of England + FCA — 2023 joint research on systemic risk detection
- Hazy (London) — synthetic data tools for financial services
- Autonomous vehicle companies — synthetic driving scenarios at scale

**The real limitations:** Synthetic data reproduces biases in the original data. It doesn't automatically stay current as the world changes. The ICO says it's not automatically outside GDPR scope — especially if source datasets are small enough to be reverse-engineered.

For UK organisations building AI where real data access is legally or contractually restricted, it's becoming the practical default.
